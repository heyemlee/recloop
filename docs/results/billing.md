A configuration's overdelivery comes from the moments a campaign spent its whole budget, at most 1 per configuration here: one event each, not an average (margin 0: $0.01 not billed, 0 clicks billed 0; margin 0 paced: $0.95 not billed, 2 clicks billed 0).

How to read it: a click is absorbed when its slot was served while the campaign still had budget and the click came in after the budget was gone. With no margin, serving stops the moment the spend in Redis reaches the budget, and the clicks still on their way from the slots served just before are absorbed: about (users looking at the campaign's slots at that moment) × CTR clicks, whatever the budget. A margin of n clicks stops serving while n clicks' worth is left, so fewer than n clicks on their way are billed in full; the price is the budget that is never spent. Pacing spreads a campaign's spend over its window, so it reaches the end of its budget late and slowly.

## How billing stays correct

- One transaction per click (`serving/internal/billing`): claim the click's event_id in `ad_clicks` (a redelivered event stops here), lock the campaign's budget row for the day the slot was served, bill min(token price, budget left), insert the charge keyed by the impression (a second click on it stops here), add to the row. `CHECK (spent_cents <= budget_cents)` backs the arithmetic.
- The consumer commits a batch's offsets only after every click in it was handled, so a `kill -9` at any point redelivers the batch and the claims make the redelivery harmless. Token expiry is judged at the time the api received the click, so a click processed late (billing down, PostgreSQL slow) is judged as it was then.
- The Redis spend counter that ad serving reads is updated after the commit, once per click (a dedup marker in the same Lua call). An update lost to a crash is re-applied by the reconciliation every minute, with the same marker, so it can never count twice.
